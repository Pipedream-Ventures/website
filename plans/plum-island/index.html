Lowering the down payment moves cost into the loan. These examples leave closing costs, improvements, and reserves fully funded. An unfunded gap means we are not ready to close.

A / BUILD THE CASH

A shared island fund.

Pick a monthly amount each household can sustain. Keep visiting while we learn the rental market and watch for a smaller property. The funding gap gives us a concrete target.

B / FIND FLEXIBLE TERMS

A patient seller.

Negotiate a smaller down payment or phased buyout. Ask why the seller would prefer payments over cash, and compare annual carry plus the balloon. The right terms still need a workable property.

C / SHARE MORE OWNERSHIP

A capital partner.

A seller or outside investor could own part of the property. Our two households can split the family’s remaining stake equally, but we would no longer each own 50% of the whole property. Agree on control and buyout rights.

Best starting posture: build the family fund, watch smaller year-round cottages, and look for a seller who wants a structured deal. A lease with an option to buy is another discussion, but option payments and rent credits need explicit terms; rent does not automatically become equity.

What about seller financing?

Compare the selected loan with a hypothetical seller carrying the same balance at 6%, amortized over 30 years, with a five-year balloon. No seller has offered these terms.

The balloon is the remaining loan balance due at year five. We would need a credible refinance, sale, or cash payoff plan.
Equity grows differently from cash

Principal repayment builds equity but cannot pay a repair bill. No appreciation, tax savings, renovation value uplift, or resale profit is included. Selling costs and market changes can reduce equity.

04 / Make 50–50 feel fair

Family first.
Clear agreement.

The working idea is 50% for Seth + Morgan and 50% for Mom + Dad. Equal starting contributions and equal shares of ongoing cash needs or distributions. The final legal ownership and financing need to match that intent.

01 · OWN IT

One property account.

Discuss a property LLC with the lender and Massachusetts attorney before buying. Rent flows into its account. Bills and reserves come out. Then distribute any available cash 50–50.

02 · RUN IT

Pay for the real work.

Agree on Morgan's renovation scope, budget, and compensation. Name a booking manager and local maintenance contact. Ownership does not mean unlimited unpaid labor.

03 · ENJOY IT

Split the good weeks fairly.

The calculator divides summer weeks equally. Agree on a rotating calendar, guests, cleaning, and how extra personal use gets charged. Revisit the rules together each year.

  • Decisions & deadlocksOne day-to-day manager within an agreed budget. Both households approve a sale, refinance, or major project. Name a tie-breaking process.
  • Repairs & additional cashSet the reserve floor and spending authority. Agree what happens if one household cannot fund its half.
  • A way outWrite down valuation, buyout timing, first purchase rights, and what happens after death, divorce, or a change in plans.

And the pub?

Keep it as a second venture with its own operating entity, budget, and local operator. An existing business with financial records may give us a clearer starting point.
